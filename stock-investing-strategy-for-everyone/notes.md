## 🏛️ 2. The Federal Reserve Chessboard (Recap)

[Watch Section](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=115)

- The Fed controls market liquidity with **two tools**:
  1. **Federal Reserve interest rate**
  2. **Federal Reserve balance sheet**

### 📉 Interest Rate Impact

#### When Interest Rate is LOW

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=172)

**Implication 1: Market valuations go up**
- Valuation metrics (P/E ratio, EV/EBITDA multiple) rise because **WACC drops**:
  - **Cost of debt** drops → companies pay less interest on borrowing
  - **Risk-free rate** (US Treasury yield) drops → **cost of equity** drops
- Lower WACC → future cash flows discounted less → higher company value
  - Example: $100 cash flow next year @ 10% WACC = $90.9 today; @ 8% WACC = $92.6 today
- Higher company value with fixed earnings → **higher P/E and EV/EBITDA multiples**
- Lower bond interest → investors shift from bonds to **stocks** → higher demand → higher valuation

**Implication 2: Companies' debt burden is reduced**
- Existing debt becomes cheaper to service; new debt is attractive to take on
- **Biggest beneficiaries**: companies that are **rapidly growing/expanding** with lots of debt and little profit
- (Companies that aren't growing and are full of debt also benefit — but don't invest in those)

#### When Interest Rate is HIGH

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=417)

- **Opposite** of low rates:
  - Market valuations go down (WACC goes higher)
  - Investors shift from stocks back to bonds
  - **Beneficiaries**: companies with **high profit and cash flow** (high growth is always good)

### 📊 Fed Balance Sheet Impact

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=438)

#### Balance Sheet INCREASING (QE)

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=444)

- Fed buys assets (mostly Treasury bonds and mortgage-backed securities) → **quantitative easing**
- Financial institutions receive cash → become extremely eager to lend
- **Easier to borrow** money
- **Borrowing rate becomes very low**

#### Balance Sheet DECREASING (QT)

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=512)

- Fed drains money by selling bonds to financial institutions or letting them mature → **quantitative tapering**
- **Opposite** of QE:
  - Harder to borrow money
  - Borrowing rate goes higher

## 🏢 3. The Four Stock Archetypes

[Watch Section](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=544)

Four key metrics to look at when picking a stock:
1. **Revenue growth**
2. **Earnings growth**
3. **Forward price-to-earnings ratio**
4. **Debt-to-EBITDA ratio**

> These are illustrative example companies to show how to play stocks in accordance with the Fed environment.

### Metric Details

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=590)

- **Revenue growth**: straightforward
- **Earnings growth**: next year's earnings ÷ last year's earnings
- **Forward P/E ratio**: ⚠️ Do **NOT** use trailing P/E
  - Use **expected future earnings** (e.g., in early 2025, use 2025 year-end expected earnings, not 2024)
  - Reason 1: Investors are **forward-looking**
  - Reason 2: Historical earnings can be distorted by one-off earnings/losses
- **Debt-to-EBITDA**: measures how much **leverage** a company has
  - Leverage has become increasingly important given drastic monetary/fiscal policies
  - Debt level drastically determines valuation given interest rates and ability to refinance
  - EBITDA = most representative metric of operations (adds back interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization)
  - Think of it as the **free cash flow** the company generates each year
  - Debt ÷ EBITDA = how many years to pay down debt

## ♟️ 4. Strategy by Fed Quadrant

[Watch Section](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=834)

### Quadrant 4 (Least Liquid): High Rate + Decreasing Balance Sheet

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=834)

- Very little liquidity in the market; risky time to invest
- **Investment**: Max **20%** of cash, or sit on the sideline
- **Stock**: Company **A**
- Why:
  - Low leverage keeps the company **solvent**
  - Low valuation (10x) means rising WACC has less impact
  - High-multiple stocks trigger **selloff mentality** when rates rise → huge drops

### Quadrant 3 (Moderate): Low Rate + Decreasing Balance Sheet

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=933)

- Some liquidity, but not always there → invest **selectively**
- **Investment**: Max **50%** of cash, or sit on the sideline
- **Stock**: Company **C** (preferred) or **B**
- Why:
  - High growth will be valued in the market
  - High leverage benefits from **decreasing interest rates** (refinance existing loans, no need to borrow new money)
  - High valuation justified as long as revenue growth is maintained in a low-rate (falling WACC) environment

### Quadrant 2 (Moderate): High Rate + Increasing Balance Sheet

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=1025)

- Similar to Quadrant 3, but interest rate is high
- **Investment**: Max **50%** of cash, or sit on the sideline
- **Stock**: Company **B** (preferred) or **C**
- Why:
  - Moderate growth + moderate leverage → relatively okay under high rates
  - P/E not as high as Company C's → impact from rising WACC is controlled

### Quadrant 1 (Most Liquid): Low Rate + Increasing Balance Sheet (QE)

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=1102)

- Fed decreasing rates + QE at the same time
- **Investment**: **100%+** of cash, aggressively — even **take on loans** to invest
- **Stock**: Company **D**
- Why:
  - Companies rarely go bankrupt in this environment → debt doesn't matter (refinancing is easy)
  - Financing costs are extremely low → no issues paying interest
  - Liquidity **hunts for growth** → money shifts from bonds to stocks looking for fast money

#### ⚠️ THE Critical Caveat: Exit at the Right Time

[Watch](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=1178)

- **Sell immediately** at any signs of:
  - Interest rates going back up
  - Quantitative easing ending
- **Don't get swayed** by media or "to the moon" Wall Street narratives
- **Take profits** at 20%, 50%, 100%, 200% — all great. Just hit the sell button at the first sign of change

## 🎯 5. Strategy Wrap-Up

[Watch Section](https://youtu.be/len22DXc8AQ?t=1223)

1. **Ideal stock**: Company D's growth + Company A's valuation and leverage ratio = **best in all scenarios**
   - If you can find a stock like that, go for it — but there aren't many out there
2. **Compare against same-sector peers**
   - Check if the company's valuation/growth is lower or higher versus peer companies
   - Take relative valuation into consideration when investing
3. **Don't chase fast money** — the more you chase it, the more it runs away
   - You don't need to hold for 20 years, but say bye-bye to your cash for **6 months to 2 years**
   - It may go down short-term, but if you did your homework you have a chance of succeeding down the road
4. **This is the creator's strategy** — it may or may not work for you
   - If you understood the points correctly, use the information to make **your own** investment decisions

## 📝 Key Takeaways

1. **Fed policies drive everything**: Monitor both the interest rate and the balance sheet
2. **Low rates + QE** → go aggressive on explosive-growth stocks (but time your exit!)
3. **High rates + QT** → stay defensive, favor stable low-debt value stocks
4. **Always use forward P/E** and check debt/EBITDA for leverage
5. **Compare within the same sector** — relative valuation matters
6. **Don't chase fast money** — hold for 6 months to 2 years minimum
7. **Build your own playbook** — no single strategy is right for everyone
